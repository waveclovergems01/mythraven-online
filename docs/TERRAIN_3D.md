## Scale and layout

- Terrain: 500 × 500 units on X/Z, Y up. Treat one unit as one metre for future content. Scale is a project convention, independent of screen pixels.
- Original world: 15,360 × 11,536 logical pixels. Original art plan: 1,448 × 1,086. Do not pass old pixel positions directly to Three.js.
- Original shoreline: `/assets/maps/mythraven-land.png`, used only as an alpha mask. Its full plan occupies 500 × 375 units, centred inside the square. Two 62.5-unit northern/southern margins extend boundary land and river exits. No X/Z stretching of the original plan.
- One 384 × 384 segment heightfield, 148,225 vertices, 294,912 top-surface triangles. Bank distance is sampled in world units from both sides of the old shoreline.
- Water: Y = 0. River bed: approximately −6. Land plateau: approximately +3.5, with gentle variation and slightly higher relief outside the city. Display cut edge: down to −13.
- Geometry and material placement are deterministic. The four owner-supplied 2026-11-13 painted grass, dry-grass, earth and grass/earth PNGs replace the old 3D ground samples. Owner correction 2026-11-14 uses ordinary full-image repeat tiling at 16 world units per image, all in a fixed X/Z orientation, replacing the swirled random patch blending; a rotated, warped paint mask gives soft grass/soil transitions at approximately 80% blended grass weight over exposed land. Source images are preserved byte-for-byte. Procedural gravel and strong grass relief are removed. Earth continues down the banks with gradual wet darkening. Upper riverbank lips retain separate upright 3D grass clumps. Depth-tinted transparent water exposes the shallow bed and uses animated ripple normals and highlights; reduced motion freezes water and grass wind. Asset mapping and cleanup audit: `docs/art/ASSET_SPEC.md`, Owner-supplied painted terrain.

## Character and navigation

The owner novice stands 2.8 units tall, with the existing 0.65-unit navigation radius and ground ring. Spawn is the nearest safe cell to old-plan (740, 900), on the southern city approach. Geometry, material colors and clips come from the supplied GLB. Exploration speed is intentionally exaggerated, so run cadence is bounded rather than forcing a five-times-speed leg cycle. No wardrobe, combat, cross-rig retargeting or legacy sprite replacement is introduced.

Navigation uses the same 384 × 384 terrain cells. Every cell corner must clear the riverbank by 4 units plus the body radius. A flood from the spawn keeps only its reachable island. Direct clear paths are used in open areas; the existing shared eight-direction A* handles detours without diagonal corner-cutting. Rendering samples the actual terrain triangles for foot height. Simulation positions/routes are independent of Three.js objects. There are no dynamic bodies or physics interactions in this stage.

## Implementation

`shared/terrain-data.mjs` owns units and elevation generation; `shared/terrain-movement.mjs` owns navigation and movement. Browser adapters in `src/terrain` load the same shoreline mask; the server reads it with Sharp. Immutable navigation is cached once per terrain, with independent movement state for each player. `server/terrain-room.mjs` validates commands, coalesces bursts to one path calculation per player per tick, assigns safe separated spawns and broadcasts numbered acknowledgements. `src/network/terrain-client.ts` sends clicks immediately and owns connection lifecycle; `terrain-prediction.ts` handles local prediction, replay and reconciliation. `terrain-mesh.ts` and `novice-player.ts` create render objects. `TerrainWorld.ts` owns raycasts, fixed follow camera, nameplates, render interpolation and cleanup. Its read-only snapshot supports tests.

This is the shared-world foundation for further MMO systems, not a claim of massive-scale readiness. Display names are cosmetic and do not authenticate account ownership. Persistent characters, account-to-game authorization, inventory/combat, reconnect resume, cross-zone travel, distributed hosting, and sustained load testing remain future work. No database, deployment or new cloud service is introduced.
